1. PURPOSE AND SCOPE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

This document defines the end-to-end incident management process for NexaCore Technologies, covering both technical incidents (outages, performance degradation) and security incidents (breaches, threats). It applies to all engineering, DevOps, and security personnel.

The goal is to minimize incident impact, restore service quickly, and ensure every incident drives improvement.

Cross-reference: Deployment SOP (ENG-SOP-007), Cybersecurity Guidelines (SEC-POL-002), Customer Support Process (SUP-PROC-001)

2. INCIDENT CLASSIFICATION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

SEV-1 (Critical):
- Complete production outage or data breach
- Affecting all or majority of users/clients
- Immediate financial or reputational risk
- Examples: Full service down, confirmed data breach, ransomware, DDoS

SEV-2 (High):
- Major functionality degraded
- Significant subset of users/clients affected
- No immediate resolution path
- Examples: Checkout failures, payment processing errors, major API degradation

SEV-3 (Medium):
- Non-critical functionality impaired
- Limited user impact with workaround available
- Examples: Reports not generating, non-critical integration failure

SEV-4 (Low):
- Minor bug or cosmetic issue
- No user impact to core flows
- Examples: UI glitch, typo in error message, low-priority integration issue

3. INCIDENT RESPONSE ROLES
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Incident Commander (IC):
- Owns the incident end-to-end
- Makes decisions when consensus cannot be reached quickly
- Delegates tasks to responders
- Communicates status to stakeholders

Technical Lead:
- Drives the technical investigation and resolution
- Coordinates with engineers across domains
- Reports findings to IC

Communications Lead:
- Handles internal and external communication
- Updates status page (status.nexacore.com)
- Drafts client notifications with Client Success

Scribe:
- Documents all actions, findings, and timeline in real-time
- Maintains the incident log in Confluence (INC space)

Subject Matter Experts (SMEs):
- Called in based on affected domain (cloud, security, DB, application)

For SEV-1: IC must be a Senior Engineer or above. On-call manager is looped in within 15 minutes.
For SEV-2: IC can be any engineer; manager notified within 1 hour.
For SEV-3/4: Standard on-call process; no formal IC required.

4. INCIDENT LIFECYCLE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

PHASE 1: DETECTION
Incidents may be detected via:
- Automated monitoring alerts (Datadog, CloudWatch, PagerDuty)
- Client reports via support portal or Tier 3 Slack
- Internal employee reports
- Security tools (GuardDuty, Sentinel, EDR)

First person to detect an incident must:
1. Immediately post in #incidents Slack channel
2. Page on-call engineer via PagerDuty
3. Open an incident ticket in Jira (project: INC)

PHASE 2: TRIAGE
IC is identified and accepts the incident within:
- SEV-1: 5 minutes
- SEV-2: 15 minutes
- SEV-3/4: 1 hour

IC creates an incident Slack channel: #inc-YYYYMMDD-[description]
All incident communication happens in this dedicated channel.
Scribe begins logging immediately.

PHASE 3: INVESTIGATION
Technical Lead begins investigation:
- Review monitoring dashboards (Datadog, CloudWatch)
- Review recent deployments (check Deployment Registry — were any in the last 2 hours?)
- Check for infrastructure alerts (AWS Health Dashboard)
- Review logs (ELK Stack / CloudWatch Logs)
- Identify blast radius: how many users/clients affected?

IC focuses on coordination — not investigation. Do not let IC get pulled into technical work.

PHASE 4: MITIGATION
Priority is to reduce impact, not necessarily to fully fix the root cause.
Mitigation options (in order of preference):
1. Rollback to last stable deployment (if deployment-related)
2. Feature flag to disable affected feature
3. Traffic rerouting or failover
4. Scale up resources (if capacity-related)
5. Emergency patch (last resort — high risk during incident)

IC authorizes mitigation action. All actions are logged by Scribe.

PHASE 5: RESOLUTION
Incident is resolved when:
- Service is restored to normal operation
- Key metrics (error rate, latency, availability) are within normal thresholds for at least 30 minutes

IC declares resolution and posts in #incidents and dedicated channel.

PHASE 6: COMMUNICATION
During the incident, Communications Lead manages:
- Internal updates (Slack #incidents every 30 min for SEV-1)
- External status page updates (status.nexacore.com)
- Client notifications (per escalation thresholds in Customer Support Process)
- Executive brief for SEV-1 (VP Engineering, CEO briefed within 1 hour)

5. POST-INCIDENT REVIEW (PIR)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

All SEV-1 and SEV-2 incidents require a Post-Incident Review (PIR), also known as a postmortem.

Timeline:
- PIR meeting: Within 48 hours of resolution (schedule immediately)
- PIR document published: Within 5 business days

PIR Document must include (use template: INC-TMPL-PIR in Confluence):
- Incident timeline (detection → resolution)
- Root cause analysis (5 Whys or similar)
- Customer/business impact (quantified where possible)
- What went well
- What went wrong
- Action items with owners and due dates

PIR culture: Blameless. Focus on process and system failures, not individuals. PIR findings are shared with the broader engineering team monthly.

6. SECURITY INCIDENT SPECIFICS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

For suspected or confirmed security breaches:
- Security team lead assumes IC role immediately
- Legal and Compliance must be notified within 1 hour
- Evidence preservation is mandatory — DO NOT wipe logs or systems
- Client notification timeline depends on contract SLA and regulatory requirements (HIPAA: 60 days; GDPR: 72 hours)
- Forensic investigation may be required (external firm may be engaged)
- Law enforcement notification: Legal team decides in coordination with CISO

For full security incident procedures, refer to the Security Incident Response Plan (SEC-SOP-001 — document currently under revision, ETA Q1 2024).

7. MONITORING AND ALERTING REFERENCE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Primary monitoring stack:
- Datadog: APM, infrastructure metrics, logs, dashboards
- PagerDuty: On-call scheduling and alerting
- CloudWatch: AWS-native metrics and alarms
- AWS GuardDuty: Threat detection
- New Relic: Secondary APM (legacy services — being migrated to Datadog)

Alert thresholds (SEV-1 triggers):
- Error rate > 5% sustained for 2+ minutes
- P99 latency > 5 seconds sustained for 2+ minutes
- Health check failure for 3+ consecutive checks
- CPU > 95% sustained for 5+ minutes across a service
